# Multi-cohort storyline options

## A. Small diagnosis-associated fractions across independent cohorts — CONDITIONAL GO (rank 1)

Novelty: turns a single-cohort robustness result into a cross-study effect-size question. Evidence needed: anchor plus at least two independent A cohorts with defensible a priori labels, cohort-specific R² and dispersion qualification. Confounders: different clinical contrasts, treatment, low-biomass processing, country/age and group imbalance. Likely figures: cohort provenance; per-cohort forest without pooled estimate; metric/dispersion matrix; cluster stability. Failure mode: effects are not comparable or one external metadata map fails. The current anchor supports the hypothesis but cannot establish consistency.

## B. Representation and study context outweigh disease labels — CONDITIONAL GO (rank 2)

Novelty: compares how feature space, geometry, QC population and study context change bounded diagnosis estimates. Evidence needed: at least three cohorts with the same prespecified sensitivity grammar and transparent technical metadata. Confounders: classifier database, host depletion and feature counts can be indistinguishable from study. Publication strength is high if technical dependencies recur without post-hoc tuning. Failure mode: external datasets cannot support equivalent feature grids, making 'outweigh' quantitatively unsupported. Prefer 'materially influence' unless evidence directly compares magnitudes.

## C. Cohort-specific patterns fail to form stable cross-study ecotypes — NO-GO as primary framing

Novelty could be strong, but evidence burden is highest: at least three sufficiently large cohorts, stable within-cohort cluster diagnostics, a prospectively specified cross-study mapping, and protection from study-driven clusters. The anchor shows poor cross-metric agreement under one tested design, not proof that ecotypes do not exist. Failure is likely because noncomparable specimen processing and diagnoses can create or erase clusters. At most this may become a bounded secondary result: no metric-stable solution under the tested representations.
