# Target Journal And Short Validation Strategy

## Decision Summary

Target a clinical Q3-compatible or moderate-threshold clinical/translational journal with a short cycle. The strongest manuscript version is:

> Public BALF mNGS re-analysis plus a small qPCR-based validation module for `Pseudomonas aeruginosa` and `Aspergillus fumigatus`, with culture/plate count only as a conventional supportive bacterial-load readout if local clinical workflow already permits it.

Do not make antimicrobial-resistance claims. Do not make clinical diagnostic-performance claims unless a separate validation cohort and analysis are added.

## Journal Priority

### Priority 1: Frontiers in Medicine

Fit:

- Broad clinical medicine scope.
- Suitable if framed as translational bioinformatics, pulmonary infection, and public-data re-analysis.
- Better if submitted to a suitable specialty section or Research Topic.

Manuscript angle:

- "BALF mNGS pathogen-marker prioritization for pulmonary infection categories."
- Include public-data re-analysis plus targeted qPCR validation if available.

Risk:

- APC and editorial scope screening.
- Needs stronger clinical relevance wording than a pure bioinformatics note.

Verdict:

- Good first-line target if the goal is speed and clinical framing.

### Priority 2: Journal of Clinical Medicine

Fit:

- Broad clinical/pre-clinical clinical medicine scope.
- Can accept clinical bioinformatics and translational validation if written around pulmonary diagnosis and clinical utility.

Manuscript angle:

- "Clinical re-analysis of BALF mNGS data prioritizes pathogen markers for rapid validation."

Risk:

- Needs a clinical rationale and preferably a validation component.
- Public-data-only manuscript may be judged too descriptive unless the group analysis and validation plan are very clear.

Verdict:

- Good if qPCR validation can be added or if the manuscript is strengthened as a concise clinical-data re-analysis.

### Priority 3: Infectious Disease Reports or similar infectious-disease brief-report journals

Fit:

- Infectious disease scope is directly aligned with pulmonary pathogens.
- Better target than broad environmental-health journals if validation is short and clinical.

Manuscript angle:

- "Re-analysis of public BALF mNGS identifies validation-priority pathogens in pulmonary infection."

Risk:

- Journal level may be lower than desired depending on indexing/quartile.

Verdict:

- Practical fallback if Q3 clinical targets reject for novelty.

### Conditional: Environmental-health route

Fit:

- Only use this route if reframed as environmental or One Health microbiome surveillance, which is not the current strongest story.
- Current dataset is clinical BALF, not environmental exposure or surveillance data.

Possible journals:

- Frontiers in Environmental Health.
- Environmental Health Insights.

Risk:

- Scope mismatch unless adding environmental exposure, hospital environment, One Health, or surveillance framing.

Verdict:

- Not first-line for the current manuscript. Use only if the paper is reframed around mNGS surveillance methodology rather than clinical BALF validation.

### Not preferred: Balkan Medical Journal

Current official pages describe Balkan Medical Journal as a Q1 general medicine journal. That makes it a higher-threshold option than the requested "clinical Q3" target.

Verdict:

- Do not prioritize for this short-cycle version.

## Recommended Submission Route

### Route A: Fastest defensible version, no wet-lab results yet

Manuscript type:

- Short communication or brief report.

Core content:

- 400-run public BALF mNGS re-analysis.
- Diagnosis-group reconstruction.
- Candidate pathogen enrichment.
- Deep-review stability.
- Host-AMR negative guardrail.

Main limitation:

- Candidate prioritization only; no wet-lab validation.

Best target:

- Frontiers in Medicine if framed clinically.
- Infectious Disease Reports or similar if Frontiers/JCM require validation.

### Route B: Stronger short-cycle version with minimal validation

Manuscript type:

- Original research / brief report.

Add validation:

- `Pseudomonas aeruginosa` qPCR/ddPCR.
- `Aspergillus fumigatus` qPCR/ddPCR.
- Optional `Cryptococcus neoformans` if fungal-positive sample numbers are adequate.
- Keep `Mycobacterium tuberculosis` as bioinformatic unless existing biosafety-approved workflow is already in place.

Main validation endpoint:

- Detection-rate concordance by clinical group.
- Directional agreement with public mNGS prioritization.

Best target:

- Journal of Clinical Medicine.
- Frontiers in Medicine.

## Short Wet-Lab Validation Plan

### Minimal panel

| Target | Suggested readout | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `Pseudomonas aeruginosa` | qPCR/ddPCR marker such as `oprL` or `ecfX` | Core bacterial validation |
| `Aspergillus fumigatus` | fungal ITS/28S qPCR/ddPCR | Core fungal validation |
| `Cryptococcus neoformans` | ITS qPCR/ddPCR | Optional fungal secondary target |
| `Mycobacterium tuberculosis` | only approved existing TB workflow | Bioinformatic endpoint unless approved |

### Plate count / culture-based supportive readout

Use only for routine bacterial targets where local clinical-lab biosafety and sample handling are already approved.

Best use:

- Support local `P. aeruginosa` positivity or bacterial burden in a subset.
- Report as concordance/supportive evidence, not as the primary public-data validation endpoint.

Do not use this route for:

- Tuberculosis.
- Fungal targets unless the lab already has routine approved workflows.
- Any experiment that would require new high-risk pathogen handling permissions.

### Minimal local sample design

Preferred:

- BALF bacterial infection group.
- BALF fungal infection group.
- BALF lung cancer disease-control group.

Minimum practical version:

- `P. aeruginosa` qPCR/ddPCR in bacterial infection vs non-bacterial BALF.
- `A. fumigatus` qPCR/ddPCR in fungal infection vs non-fungal BALF.

Avoid:

- Healthy-control claims if no true healthy BALF samples exist.
- Diagnostic accuracy claims unless a prespecified validation cohort and analysis are available.

## Revised Manuscript Claim With Validation

If qPCR validation is added:

> Public BALF mNGS re-analysis prioritized diagnosis-associated pathogen markers, and targeted qPCR validation in local BALF samples supported the feasibility of validating `P. aeruginosa` and `A. fumigatus` as short-cycle pulmonary infection markers.

If no validation is added:

> Public BALF mNGS re-analysis prioritized diagnosis-associated pathogen markers and produced a focused validation panel for short-cycle translational follow-up.

## Immediate Operational Next Step

Prepare two manuscript variants:

1. Public-data-only version for immediate submission or pre-submission inquiry.
2. Public-data-plus-minimal-qPCR version for a stronger JCM/Frontiers submission if validation can be completed quickly.

- Frontiers in Medicine official scope describes it as a broad, multidisciplinary clinical medicine journal.
- Journal of Clinical Medicine official page describes it as an international peer-reviewed open-access journal covering clinical and pre-clinical research.
- Frontiers in Environmental Health and Environmental Health Insights are environment-health aligned, but the current clinical BALF dataset needs reframing before these become good fits.
