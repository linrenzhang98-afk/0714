# Discussion skeleton

## Opening branch — select exactly one after formal analysis

### STRONG

Both cohort-specific analyses show material, representation-robust community organization with acceptable dispersion behavior. State both effect sizes and uncertainties, name the different clinical contrasts in every synthesis sentence, and conclude only that community-level ecological organization was informative under a shared measurement grammar.

### MODEST

At least one primary association is small but coherent across prespecified representations and secondary ecology. Lead with bounded effect magnitude and substantial within-group heterogeneity. Frame the common contribution as reproducible measurement and qualified ecological organization, not strong clinical separation.

### NULL

The prespecified analyses do not resolve clear clinical-group associations. Emphasize the informative boundary: a technically aligned classifier layer did not make heterogeneous clinical contrasts strongly explanatory. Do not infer equivalence, absence of lower-airway biology or failure of all taxon-level hypotheses.

### DISPERSION_QUALIFIED

At least one PERMANOVA finding is materially accompanied by unequal dispersion. Describe location and/or dispersion structure, explain that ordination separation cannot be assigned uniquely to centroids, and center conclusions on heterogeneity, representation dependence and technical ecology.

## Community-level versus taxon-level inference

Community geometry and individual taxa answer different questions. A community association can be distributed across ratios without yielding a stable taxon list; conversely, an isolated taxon result need not imply global organization. The primary design therefore prioritizes effect-size-led community inference. Sparse taxon testing, if later justified, remains exploratory and method-sensitive.

## Heterogeneous clinical contrasts

The anchor compares four published pulmonary diagnoses; the external cohort compares TB drug-resistance categories. Their effects are not exchangeable. Side-by-side behavior can demonstrate that the ecological framework remains informative—or not—under distinct pulmonary contexts, but cannot establish replication, a common clinical coefficient or formal meta-analysis.

## Classifier-dependent subcomposition and technical provenance

Direct species assignments are conditioned on the Kraken2 database, grammar and reads available to classification. The shared layer improves measurement consistency but does not harmonize extraction, host depletion, read architecture or library batch. Low and variable classified fractions further narrow inference. Because classified fraction is not bacterial biomass, any association with clinical groups must be treated as technical classifier yield and possible feature-generation confounding.

## Covariates and controls

No complete shared run-level set of age, sex, antibiotic exposure, treatment, severity and comorbidity variables was available. Cohort-specific unadjusted associations therefore remain vulnerable to clinical confounding. PRJCA046985 lacks an identified public negative-control run, which especially limits sparse taxon-level contamination claims. The common classifier layer cannot repair these design limitations.

## Third-cohort rationale

A third cohort is optional rather than mandatory because the current claim concerns two separate analyses connected by common ecological measurement. A future cohort becomes important for genuine replication only if it repeats one clinical estimand with compatible eligibility, labels and provenance. Post hoc cohort addition to obtain concordance would weaken, not strengthen, the design.
