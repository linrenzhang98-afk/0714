# Introduction draft

Shotgun metagenomic studies of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) are becoming increasingly available across pulmonary disease settings. Their joint interpretability nevertheless remains limited by cohort-specific preprocessing, taxonomic databases, assignment definitions, feature filters and ecological representations. Apparent differences between studies can therefore arise from measurement grammar as well as from clinical or biological heterogeneity. A reproducible cross-study analysis needs to make the shared measurement layer explicit without treating technical harmonization as proof of interchangeable populations.

Community-level ecological structure offers one useful level of inquiry. Individual taxon findings can be sensitive to sparsity, database coverage, contamination and multiplicity, whereas whole-community geometry asks how relative organization varies across prespecified clinical groups. This does not make community metrics universally transportable, but it permits effect-size and dispersion questions to be posed consistently. In particular, compositional analysis separates relative organization within the observed feature set from classifier yield, which is better treated as a technical endpoint.

Here, a common native Kraken2 classifier-assignment layer provides the same database, parameters, report grammar and direct-assignment definition for two BALF shotgun cohorts. The layer standardizes what is counted; it does not erase differences in source processing, read architecture, recruitment or clinical labels. Direct species assignments remain a classifier-defined subcomposition, and classified fraction does not measure bacterial biomass.

We therefore address two linked but distinct questions. In the 400-patient PRJNA1056765 anchor cohort, we estimate the omnibus association between four published pulmonary diagnoses and BALF species composition. In the 130-subject PRJCA046985 cohort, we separately estimate the association between Drug_Resistance and Drug_Sensitive tuberculosis status and BALF species composition. We then compare ecological effect structure, dispersion, diversity, dominance, technical classifier yield and taxonomic observability under the same measurement grammar. This synthesis evaluates qualified ecological generalizability, not clinical replication, a universal disease signature or a pooled 530-sample effect.
