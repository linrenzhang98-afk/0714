# Abstract skeleton

## Background

Lower-airway shotgun datasets are increasingly available, but cohort-specific processing and heterogeneous clinical definitions limit ecological comparison. We asked whether a common native classifier-assignment grammar can support separate within-cohort community analyses and qualified cross-cohort ecological synthesis.

## Methods

We studied 400 unique PRJNA1056765 BALF patients across four published diagnoses and 130 unique PRJCA046985 BALF subjects with Drug_Resistance or Drug_Sensitive tuberculosis. Cohorts were analyzed independently using species direct-assigned Kraken2 counts, a 10% within-cohort prevalence filter, exact CZM zero replacement, CLR transformation, Aitchison distance, 9,999-permutation PERMANOVA and paired PERMDISP. Richness, Shannon, Gini-Simpson, dominance and classified fraction were secondary endpoints; 5%/20%, 0.5-pseudocount and Bray-Curtis analyses assessed robustness.
