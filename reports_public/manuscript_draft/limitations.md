# Limitations

1. The cohorts address different clinical contrasts, so neither pooled diagnosis inference nor formal meta-analysis is available.
2. The design is observational and lacks a complete common adjustment set for age, sex, antibiotics, treatment, severity and comorbidity.
3. A common Kraken2 layer aligns classifier/database/report semantics but not extraction, upstream host processing, read architecture or all batch factors.
4. Direct-species composition is a classifier-defined subcomposition. Structural absence cannot be separated from sampling/classification zeros.
5. Classified fractions are low and variable and do not measure bacterial biomass.
6. Richness and the other direct-species alpha metrics remain sensitive to sequencing and classification effort; classified fraction does not remove that depth dependence.
7. PRJCA046985 has no identified public negative-control run, limiting contamination-sensitive taxon claims.
8. Prevalence filtering, zero handling and distance representation may affect conclusions; all prespecified representations must therefore be shown.
9. Public labels and frozen split/technical variables limit the available models; missing covariates must not be reconstructed from outcome data.
10. Two cohorts can assess the operational form of ecological generalizability defined here but cannot support a universal pulmonary disease signature.
11. A third cohort would be needed for genuine replication of either clinical estimand, but is not required to answer the current two-cohort question.
