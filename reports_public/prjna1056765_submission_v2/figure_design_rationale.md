# Figure design rationale

The rebuilt architecture contains three main figures and one supplementary comparator. It replaces audit-report boxes and judgment summaries with plotted frozen data.

Figure 1 uses a compact provenance flow and diagnosis-composition bars. It separates the Han et al. n=284/118 analytical split from the current n=400/n=119 estimands without implying independent cohorts.

Figure 2 is the principal data figure. Discrete points show all twelve Aitchison cells, grouped into two explicitly different populations. P1 and P2 share a row grammar but are never connected across prevalence spaces. A common honest R² scale permits visual comparison, and a small dagger marks dispersion qualification. The near-overlap at 30 species makes pseudocount stability visible, while changes across feature spaces remain discrete.

Figure 3 displays the existing clustering diagnostics. Lines are appropriate because k is an ordered candidate sequence. The boundary maximum for Bray–Curtis and near-zero cross-metric adjusted Rand indices remain visible without declaring an optimal ecotype count. The existing PCoA was omitted because it added descriptive complexity without strengthening the frozen claim.

Supplementary Figure S1 applies the Figure 2 grammar to Bray–Curtis. It retains the complete metric comparator while keeping the main narrative focused on compositional analysis.

The frozen taxon table was not promoted to Figure 4. Its pipeline dependence, unresolved upstream equivalence, and secondary role made a main taxon reproducibility map more likely to distract from the community-level estimand than to add evidence. The complete table remains in Supplementary Table S5.

The palette uses blue, orange, green, purple, and neutral grey with redundant shapes or panel structure. White backgrounds, thin rules, direct axes, and sparse annotation support grayscale and reduced-size reading. No trend line connects prevalence-defined feature spaces, no decorative icon is used, and axes begin at zero for effect-size panels.
