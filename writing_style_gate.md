# Manuscript writing gate

Apply this checklist to every abstract sentence, result paragraph, figure legend, table note, and discussion claim.

## 1. Evidence alignment

Each claim must point to one frozen result, figure, table, or real citation. State the estimand, cohort, direction, effect size, and uncertainty when available. Remove claims whose evidence is only a plausible explanation. A method change can strengthen inference but does not make the same biological observation novel.

## 2. Non-template structure

Use headings that name the scientific point. Vary paragraph shape according to the evidence rather than repeating background–result–interpretation formulas. Avoid serial colon titles, repeated “first/second/finally” scaffolds, and recycled transition sentences. One paragraph should make one necessary move.

## 3. Lexical compression

Prefer concrete verbs and measured quantities. Replace “demonstrated the presence of a significant association” with “diagnosis explained 1.9% of Aitchison variation.” Delete redundant pairs, stacked abstractions, and inflated terms such as “robustness and reliability” when one precise term suffices. Do not use “biomarker,” “signature,” “dysbiosis,” “mechanism,” or “validation” unless the design supports that word.

## 4. Punctuation restraint

Use full stops and short declarative sentences by default. Reserve colons for true expansions, dashes for necessary interruptions, semicolons for closely linked independent clauses, and parentheses for compact secondary detail. Do not place ordinary terms in quotation marks.

## 5. Source traceability

Cite all external facts, numbers, mechanistic statements, and dataset attributes at the point of use. Distinguish explicitly among “we found,” “Han et al. reported,” and “prior lower-airway studies suggest.” Link dataset accessions and software/code versions. Never cite a review for a primary result when the primary paper is available.

## Mandatory claim labels during drafting

- **Our result:** generated by the frozen 400-run analysis and traceable to a checked-in table.
- **Prior result:** reported by a cited source, including the original PRJNA1056765 papers.
- **Inference:** interpretation consistent with evidence but not directly measured.
- **Hypothesis:** requires new analysis or data.

Any sentence that cannot be assigned one label is deleted or rewritten. Natural scientific prose is the goal; do not introduce errors or stylistic quirks to imitate human writing.
